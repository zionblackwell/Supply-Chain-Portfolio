Fresh Fork Inventory Control Center

An interactive, browser-based exploration of inventory trade-offs for a hypothetical fast-casual restaurant, paired with a downloadable Excel inventory workbook.

↓ Download Excel workbook

The problem

The scenario describes approximately 20% weekly produce waste alongside stockout risk. Over-ordering can worsen spoilage; under-ordering can disrupt service.

What the model demonstrates

Reorder-point decisions, days of cover, safety stock, illustrative spoilage flags, and expiry-aware suggested order quantities.

Try the model

Explore two independent scenarios: adjust the service-level target or expected daily demand. Watch coverage, spoilage exposure, reorder points, and purchasing recommendations respond.

Adjusts safety stock, reorder points, and purchasing recommendations.

100% = baseline demand from the workbook. 120% = 20% more daily usage; 80% = 20% less. Inventory on hand stays fixed.

What changes? Service level adjusts safety stock. Demand adjusts projected daily usage, days of cover, reorder points, spoilage flags, and suggested quantities. Current stock and shelf life remain fixed. Statuses change when a threshold is crossed; expiry caps can prevent additional purchasing.

Model assumptions & formulas

Safety stock = z(service level) × estimated daily-demand variability × √lead time. In this scenario, demand variability scales proportionally with expected demand.

Reorder point = average daily use × lead time + safety stock.

Spoilage flag = current days of cover > assumed shelf life. Estimated at-risk value = excess units × unit cost.

Order-up-to target = minimum of (reorder point + 3 days' use) and (estimated use before expiry). Suggested order = max(0, target − on hand), only when reorder is triggered.

Important: This interactive web simulation extends the attached Excel workbook with two scenario controls. The downloadable workbook provides the original inventory dashboard and project guide; the browser simulation adds service-level sensitivity. The Excel workbook contains a fixed-safety-stock reorder model; it does not currently include the website's service-level and demand sliders, demand-variability estimates, or shelf-life constraints. Baseline safety-stock values come from the workbook; illustrative shelf lives and the slider calibration are modeling assumptions, not observed operating data. Neither model is connected to a live POS or supplier. The case's ~20% waste figure is a scenario premise, not a measured result or claimed reduction.
